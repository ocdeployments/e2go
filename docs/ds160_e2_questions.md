# DS-160 questions for an E-2 visa applicant

This document consolidates the questions and question groups that appear in the DS-160 for an E-2 visa applicant, using the Department of State DS-160 exemplar to identify the E-visa-specific sections and a detailed DS-160 question guide to enumerate the underlying prompts.[cite:1][cite:2]

## Scope

The DS-160 is the standard online nonimmigrant visa application, and the Department of State exemplar shows that an E-2 principal applicant will see the standard DS-160 sections plus E-visa-specific sections such as **Travel Information (E1/E2-EX Visas)**, **E-Visa: Business Profile**, **E-Visa: Foreign Parent Business**, **E-Visa: Investment**, **E-Visa: U.S. Personnel 1**, **E-Visa: U.S. Personnel 2**, **E-Visa: Applicant Present Position**, **E-Visa: Applicant Position in U.S.**, and **E-Visa: Application Contact**.[cite:1]

Because the live DS-160 is dynamic, some questions appear only if a prior answer triggers them, such as questions about other names, other nationalities, prior U.S. travel, spouse details, or lost passports.[cite:1][cite:2]

## Travel Information for E-2 applicants

The Department of State exemplar shows a dedicated **Travel Information (E1/E2-EX Visas)** page for principal E-1/E-2 applicants, and also a **Temporary Work Visa** page that applies to E-2 classifications.[cite:1]

The exemplar does not expose every field as plain text in the summary page, but it confirms that E-2 applicants receive additional E-classification travel and employment prompts beyond the standard travel questions.[cite:1]

## Family Information: Spouse / Partner / Former Spouse / Children

## Security and Background

The exemplar confirms five separate **Security and Background** pages, and the detailed DS-160 guide notes that a “yes” answer to any of these questions can lead to additional scrutiny or denial.[cite:1][cite:2]

These pages cover health-related grounds, criminal history, controlled-substance violations, prostitution and vice, money laundering, human trafficking, immigration fraud or misrepresentation, unlawful presence, removal or deportation issues, espionage, sabotage, terrorism, genocide, torture, extrajudicial killings, child soldiers, and related national-security or public-safety grounds.[cite:1][cite:2]

## E-visa sections specific to E-2 cases

For a principal E-2 applicant, the Department of State exemplar confirms the following additional E-visa pages appear in the DS-160: **E-Visa: Business Profile**, **E-Visa: Foreign Parent Business**, **E-Visa: Investment**, **E-Visa: U.S. Personnel 1**, **E-Visa: U.S. Personnel 2**, **E-Visa: Applicant Present Position**, **E-Visa: Applicant Position in U.S.**, and **E-Visa: Application Contact**.[cite:1]

The exemplar also states that **E-Visa: Investment** is displayed for E2-TR and E2-EX applicants, and that **E-Visa: Applicant Present Position**, **E-Visa: Applicant Position in U.S.**, and **E-Visa: Application Contact** are displayed for all principal E-visa applicants.[cite:1]

### Business Profile

This section asks for identifying information about the treaty enterprise in the United States, including the business name and related profile details for the E enterprise.[cite:1]

### Foreign Parent Business

This section appears for treaty trader and treaty investor principal cases and asks about the foreign business/owner relationship behind the enterprise.[cite:1]

### Investment

For E-2 cases, this section asks about the investment itself, and the exemplar confirms that selecting “Other” in available fields reveals additional prompts.[cite:1]

In practice, this section is used to capture the source, amount, nature, and status of the E-2 investment and related enterprise details reflected in the DS-160 E-visa workflow.[cite:1]

### U.S. Personnel 1 and 2

These sections ask about staffing and personnel in the U.S. enterprise, and the exemplar shows alternate versions of **U.S. Personnel 2** depending on whether personnel status is nonimmigrant, lawful permanent resident, or other/unknown.[cite:1]

### Applicant Present Position

This section asks about the applicant’s current position with the company and displays additional fields when answers trigger them.[cite:1]

### Applicant Position in U.S.

This section asks about the role the applicant will hold in the United States, including whether the applicant is replacing a worker, because the exemplar shows separate flows for “replacing worker = yes” and “replacing worker = no.”[cite:1]

### Application Contact

This section asks for the contact details of the person connected to the E-visa application.[cite:1]

## Temporary Work Visa page

The exemplar separately lists a **Temporary Work Visa** page for E1-EX, E1-TR, E2-EX, and E2-TR classifications, which means E-2 applicants should also expect employer/petition-style work questions tied to the temporary work classification workflow.[cite:1]

## Practical note

A complete “all questions” list for DS-160 cannot be extracted from a single static official page because the form is conditional and shows different pages depending on visa class and prior answers.[cite:1]

For E-2 work, the most accurate way to use this document is as a comprehensive intake checklist covering the standard DS-160 core plus the E-visa-specific pages that the Department of State explicitly shows for E-2 applicants.[cite:1][cite:2]

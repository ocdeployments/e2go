## SECTION 1: HOW THE APPLICATION IS ORGANIZED

### 1.1 The Tabbed Binder Format

The E-2 application for Canadian applicants must be submitted as a **tabbed binder** — a physical or digital document package organized into labeled sections [page:0]. New cases and renewals are submitted electronically to the U.S. Consulate in Toronto via **evisacanada@state.gov** before any interview is scheduled [page:0][web:127].

The binder has a strict **50-page limit** at the Toronto Consulate. Every page counts. Documents must be organized in A4 or U.S. letter format in a three-ring binder with labeled tabs [web:123].

The official tab structure published by U.S. Embassy posts is:

| Tab | Name | Core Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Cover Letter | Argues the legal case — addresses all 6 E-2 eligibility requirements |
| **B** | Administrative Documents | Forms, photos, fees, family proof |
| **C** | Company Structure / Ownership | Proves treaty-country nationals own ≥50% |
| **D** | Proof of Investment | Proves funds have been committed and deployed at risk |
| **E** | Proof the Enterprise Operates | Proves the business is real and operational (or imminently so) |
| **F** | Proof of Substantial Investment | Proves the investment amount is substantial relative to total cost |
| **G** | Proof Investment is Not Marginal | Proves the business will generate income beyond a mere living |
| **H** | Applicant Qualifications | Proves the investor can develop and direct the enterprise |

Some consulate versions and practitioner conventions use an expanded tab structure (Tabs A–L) that further subdivides these sections. The tabs above are the official U.S. Embassy-published minimum structure.

### 1.2 Two Processing Streams at Toronto

The U.S. Consulate in Toronto processes E-2 applications in two distinct streams [web:127]:

**Stream 1 — New Cases and Renewals**
- First-time applicants and companies seeking re-registration
- Must apply exclusively in Toronto
- Submit documents electronically to evisacanada@state.gov FIRST
- Do NOT book an interview until the Consulate confirms the application is complete and schedules the interview
- Company registration (once approved) is valid for 5 years

**Stream 2 — Employees of Registered Companies and Dependents**
- Employees of a currently registered E-2 company
- Qualifying family members of a valid E-2 holder
- May schedule interviews at Toronto, Calgary, Montreal, Ottawa, or Vancouver
- Simpler document package — no full binder required

## SECTION 2: TAB-BY-TAB DOCUMENT REQUIREMENTS

---

### TAB A — COVER LETTER

**What it is:** A formal legal brief written in letter format that argues the entire E-2 case on paper. This is not a generic introduction — it is the most important narrative document in the binder.

**What it must address (all 6 are mandatory):** [page:0]

1. The investor has invested or is actively in the process of investing (per 9 FAM 402.9-6(B))
2. The enterprise is currently running or will be running in the immediate future (per 9 FAM 402.9-6(C))
3. The investment is substantial (per 9 FAM 402.9-6(D))
4. The investment is more than marginal — i.e., it is not solely for earning a living (per 9 FAM 402.9-6(E))
5. The applicant is in a position to develop and direct the enterprise (per 9 FAM 402.9-6(F))
6. The applicant intends to depart the United States when E-2 status terminates

**Additional required element:**
- If the company has previously qualified for E visa status: include the date of the initial visa issuance

**Best practices:**
- Written in formal business letter format on company letterhead where applicable
- Typically 3–6 pages
- Cites specific supporting documents by tab: "As shown in Tab D, wire transfer dated [date]..."
- Uses precise, factual language — no vague assertions
- Each of the 6 elements above gets its own paragraph or subsection
- Never makes legal conclusions the officer hasn't yet reached — it presents the facts and lets the officer conclude

**What NOT to include:**
- Emotional appeals or personal stories
- Unsupported claims ("our business will definitely succeed")
- Information not corroborated by documents elsewhere in the binder

---

### TAB B — ADMINISTRATIVE DOCUMENTS

**What it contains:** [page:0]

1. **DS-160 Confirmation Page** — printed barcode confirmation page from the completed online nonimmigrant visa application. Each applicant (including dependents) must have their own DS-160.
2. **Passport Photo** — one 5×5 cm photo taken within the last six months, white background, for each applicant and accompanying family member
3. **DS-156E Form** — the Nonimmigrant Treaty Trader / Treaty Investor Application, completed and signed. Must include a valid email address. Must be filled out in its entirety. (NOT required for dependent family members)
4. **Proof of family relationships** (if dependents are included):
   - Marriage certificate (for spouse)
   - Birth certificates (for children)
5. **MRV fee payment receipt(s)** — proof of visa application fee payment for each applicant

**Notes:**
- DS-156E is specific to treaty visa applicants — it is a separate form from DS-160
- The DS-156E asks about the applicant's business, position, and role — answers must be consistent with the rest of the binder
- Every dependent (spouse, child under 21) files their own DS-160 and requires their own photo and fee payment

---

### TAB C — COMPANY STRUCTURE AND OWNERSHIP

**What it proves:** That nationals of a treaty country (Canada) own at least 50% of the enterprise, and that a Canadian national develops and directs its activities [page:0]

**Documents required:**

For a corporation (Inc., Corp.):
- Articles of incorporation
- Stock certificates and stock ledger
- Secretary of State certificate
- Shareholder agreement (if applicable)

For an LLC (most common):
- Articles of organization or formation
- State LLC registration / Certificate of Organization
- Operating agreement (most critical — must show ownership percentages clearly)
- Member ledger or certificate

For a franchise:
- Franchise agreement (showing franchisee identity and ownership)
- Operating agreement of the U.S. entity
- EIN confirmation (IRS SS-4 or online confirmation)

For complex ownership structures (multiple owners, subsidiaries, chains):
1. An organizational chart clearly showing the full ownership structure
2. Legal proof of ownership at each level
3. Photocopies of the biodata (photo) page of each passport AND the ownership percentage of each unit holder
4. If the enterprise is incorporated outside the U.S.: a chart of ownership AND a certificate of existence/registration from the jurisdiction where incorporated

**What NOT to include:**
- Draft or unsigned documents — all corporate documents must be executed
- Generic templates that are not yet customized to the actual company

---

### TAB D — PROOF OF INVESTMENT

**What it proves:** That the funds have been invested, or that the investor is actively in the process of investing — and that money has been committed at risk to the enterprise [page:0]

**Critical rule from the U.S. Embassy:** It is important to provide proof of **actual purchases and/or signed contracts and leases** — not just wire transfers to a U.S. account [page:0]. Sitting funds in a U.S. bank account are NOT sufficient proof of investment.

**Documents to include (as many as possible):**

- Escrow documents (for business purchase or real estate for the business)
- Wire transfer confirmations (showing funds moved from Canadian account to U.S. business account)
- Lease contract — must include:
  - Lease length with start and end dates
  - Property address and size
  - Monthly rental amount
  - Landlord and tenant names
  - All required signatures
- Sales invoices for equipment, furniture, supplies, or materials purchased
- Inventory listing and shipment invoices for inventory, equipment, or business-related property
- Receipts for all improvement expenses (renovations, build-out, fit-out)
- Purchasing agreements — must include:
  - Buyer and seller names
  - Date of agreement
  - Purchase amount
  - Special conditions
- Franchise agreement — entire agreement must be included (not a summary)
- Vendor contracts, supplier agreements
- Payroll records if employees have already been hired

**What the officer is looking for:**
- A clear, traceable path: Canadian source → Canadian bank → U.S. business bank → actual purchases
- Evidence that the money is truly "at risk" — committed to the business, not sitting in a savings account
- No "round trip" funds — money sent to the U.S. and then returned to Canada is not a qualifying investment

---

### TAB E — PROOF THAT THE ENTERPRISE OPERATES

**What it proves:** That the enterprise is currently running, or will open its doors imminently [page:0]

**Documents to include:**

- Business license(s) — city, county, and state as applicable
- Special permits (food service permit, alcohol license, home care agency license, etc.)
- Utility bills (electrical, water, internet) showing the business address
- Business bank account statements
- Supplier invoices and purchase orders
- Sales contracts or client invoices (if revenue-generating)
- Advertising materials, website printout, business cards, brochures
- Signed staffing records or employment agreements (if staff have been hired)
- Franchisor confirmation of training completion (if franchise)

**Also required in Tab E:** Financial projections for the next 5 years — this may be included within a business plan [page:0]

**Timing note:**
- The business does not need to be fully operational at the time of application — but it must be "imminently" operational
- The stronger the evidence of operational status, the more credible the application
- For franchises: a signed franchise agreement, a signed lease, and a paid franchise fee is typically sufficient to demonstrate imminence even if doors are not yet open

---

### TAB F — PROOF OF SUBSTANTIAL INVESTMENT

**What it proves:** That the investment amount is substantial relative to the total cost of establishing or acquiring the business [page:0]

**The legal standard (9 FAM 402.9-6(D)):**
- No fixed minimum dollar amount
- Uses a "proportionality" or "sliding scale" test
- A smaller business requires a higher percentage to be invested; a larger business may qualify with a lower percentage
- The key question: is the investment enough to ensure the investor's commitment to the business's success?

**Documents to include:**
- A detailed capital investment schedule or use-of-funds table:
  - Total cost to establish or acquire the business
  - Amount invested by the applicant to date
  - Amount remaining to be invested and on what timeline
  - Breakdown by category: franchise fee, equipment, lease deposits, fit-out, working capital, professional fees
- Receipts, invoices, and purchase records for funds already spent (these overlap with Tab D)
- Bank statements showing the current balance of the remaining investment capital
- If a portion of funds is not yet deployed: a signed letter from the investor confirming the remaining funds are committed, with supporting account statements

**What NOT to present:**
- Investment that is primarily working capital sitting in a bank account — this weakens proportionality
- Loans secured against the business (as opposed to against the investor's personal assets) — these do not count as qualifying investment

---

### TAB G — PROOF THAT INVESTMENT IS NOT MARGINAL

**What it proves:** That the enterprise is more than marginal — meaning it will generate income beyond merely supporting the investor's household, and will make a meaningful contribution to the U.S. economy [page:0]

**The legal standard (9 FAM 402.9-6(E)):**
- The business must generate income beyond what is needed for the investor and their family to live on
- Job creation is the most powerful evidence of non-marginality
- A business that only supports the investor and has no employees will face heavy scrutiny
- Projected income must exceed the investor's household income need by a factor typically interpreted as 3–4x

**Required documents:**

For ALL applicants:
- A **5-year business plan** prepared by (or certified by) a CPA, including projected expenses and profits [page:0]
- For new businesses: the plan must verify the capacity of the enterprise to realize a profit within a maximum of 5 years

For existing businesses:
- Pages 1–2 of the appropriate federal tax forms for the most recent year (showing actual revenue and profitability)
- Business bank statements
- Financial statements (P&L, balance sheet)

**What makes a strong non-marginality case:**
- Projected Year 1 revenue is at least 3–4x the investor's stated household income need
- A documented hiring plan with specific positions, timelines, and wage rates
- Evidence of existing or signed client contracts
- Territory market data showing demand exists

---

### TAB H — APPLICANT QUALIFICATIONS

**What it proves:** That the applicant is capable of and positioned to develop and direct the enterprise [page:0]

**Documents required:**
1. Applicant's resume — including all relevant educational and work experience
2. Organizational chart — showing the applicant's position in the company and any direct subordinates
3. If someone is being replaced by the applicant: clearly identify who

**For previously U.S.-based applicants:**
- Pages 1–2 of IRS Form 1040 for the three most recent fiscal years

**For applicants applying as a Treaty Employee (executive/supervisory):**
- Evidence meeting 9 FAM 402.9-7(B) criteria
- Job description showing the executive or supervisory nature of the role
- Organizational chart showing subordinate employees

**For applicants applying as an Essential Employee:**
- Evidence the employee has essential skills the enterprise urgently needs
- Projected duration of this essentiality
- Relevant diplomas, job training certificates, or employer letters
- Explanation of why the enterprise was unable to find a qualified U.S. citizen or LPR for the position
- Must address criteria in 9 FAM 402.9-7(C)

## SECTION 3: THE BUSINESS PLAN — FULL CONTENTS AND STANDARDS

### 3.1 Why the Business Plan Is the Most Important Document

Of all documents in the E-2 binder, the business plan carries the most narrative weight [web:128]. It must simultaneously:
- Prove the investment is substantial and at risk
- Prove the business is not marginal
- Demonstrate the investor can develop and direct the enterprise
- Show the business is viable and realistic
- Prove the investor understands the market and the business they are buying

A generic template downloaded from the internet will not work. Officers are experienced at identifying plans that are vague, internally inconsistent, or clearly written by someone who does not understand the business [web:128].

### 3.2 What the Business Plan Must Accomplish — The 5 Jobs

1. **Prove the investment is substantial and at risk:** Show exactly how much has been invested, where it went, and why that amount represents a meaningful commitment relative to the total cost of the enterprise
2. **Prove the business is NOT marginal:** Show projections demonstrating revenue well beyond the investor's household income need, with a documented path to job creation and economic impact
3. **Prove the investor will direct and develop the enterprise:** Show the investor's specific role, daily responsibilities, decision-making authority, and why their background makes them the right operator
4. **Prove the business is viable:** Ground projections in market data, competitive analysis, and realistic revenue assumptions — not aspirational guesswork
5. **Tell a coherent story:** The executive summary, the operations plan, the financial model, and the qualifications section must align perfectly. Internal inconsistencies sink applications [web:128]

### 3.3 Required Structure and Contents

**SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1–2 pages)**
- Business name, legal structure (LLC, Corp.), state of formation
- Nature of the business: what product or service is provided, to whom, where
- Investment amount and source (brief)
- Franchise system (if applicable): brand name, parent company, years in business
- Investor name, nationality, proposed role in the business
- Brief summary of why this business is a strong E-2 candidate
- Key financial highlights: projected Year 1 revenue, Year 3 revenue, Year 1 employees, Year 3 employees

**SECTION 2: COMPANY DESCRIPTION (1–2 pages)**
- Full legal name and DBA (if any)
- State of incorporation/formation and date
- Federal EIN
- Registered address and operating address
- Ownership structure: investor name, ownership percentage
- Type of business: franchise vs. independent, new vs. acquisition
- Franchise details (if applicable):
  - Franchisor name and corporate address
  - Franchise agreement execution date
  - Territory description
  - Initial franchise fee paid
  - Ongoing royalty and fee structure
- Business mission and value proposition

**SECTION 3: PRODUCTS AND SERVICES (1–2 pages)**
- Detailed description of what the business sells or provides
- Service delivery model: how services are provided, by whom, at what price
- Pricing structure and pricing rationale
- Service differentiation: what makes this business competitive
- For franchises: how the franchisor's system, brand, and training support the investor

**SECTION 4: MARKET ANALYSIS (2–4 pages)**
- Target market definition: who are the customers? (age, income, geography, need)
- Market size: total addressable market in the territory (number of potential customers/clients)
- Local demand evidence: why this territory needs this business
- Demographic data: population, income levels, target cohort size (e.g., 65+ for senior care)
- Growth trends: is the market expanding?
- Competitive analysis:
  - Who are the direct competitors in this territory?
  - What are their strengths and weaknesses?
  - What is the competitive gap that this business will fill?
  - How does the franchisor brand differentiate from local competitors?

**SECTION 5: MARKETING AND CUSTOMER ACQUISITION STRATEGY (1–2 pages)**
- How will the business acquire its first clients?
- Marketing channels: digital marketing, referral networks, community outreach, franchisor marketing support
- Initial marketing budget and timeline
- Sales strategy: B2B outreach (if applicable), B2C advertising, networking
- Customer retention strategy: how will the business keep clients long-term?

**SECTION 6: OPERATIONS PLAN (2–3 pages)**
- Location: address (or description if not yet finalized), lease terms
- Hours of operation
- Staffing plan:
  - Owner/investor role: specific title and day-to-day responsibilities
  - Initial staff roster: positions, roles, start dates, wages
  - Year 1–3 hiring plan: how many employees, in what roles, at what wages, by when
  - Total projected payroll by Year 1 and Year 3
- Equipment and supplies: what is needed to operate, what has been purchased
- Technology systems: software, point-of-sale, scheduling, CRM
- Franchisor support: training programs, field support, national marketing fund
- Licensing and permits: what is required and status of applications

**SECTION 7: MANAGEMENT AND ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE (1–2 pages)**
- Investor biography: work history, management experience, industry knowledge, relevant education
- Why the investor is qualified to run this specific business
- Organizational chart: investor at the top, with reporting lines to all employees
- Key positions and responsibilities
- If a general manager will be hired: their role, authority, and the investor's retained directorial responsibilities

**SECTION 8: FINANCIAL PLAN (4–6 pages — the core of the plan)**

This section is the most scrutinized by officers. It must be internally consistent, grounded in real data, and prepared or certified by a CPA [page:0].

*8.1 — Use of Funds / Capital Deployment Table*
A line-by-line breakdown of how every dollar of the investment has been or will be spent:

| Category | Amount |
|---|---|
| Franchise fee | $X |
| Leasehold improvements / build-out | $X |
| Equipment and fixtures | $X |
| Initial inventory | $X |
| Technology and software | $X |
| Professional fees (legal, CPA, immigration) | $X |
| Initial marketing and advertising | $X |
| Working capital (operating reserve) | $X |
| **Total Investment** | **$X** |

*8.2 — Revenue Projections (5 Years)*
- Monthly revenue projections for Year 1
- Annual revenue projections for Years 2–5
- Revenue assumptions clearly stated:
  - Number of clients or transactions per month
  - Average revenue per client / per transaction
  - Growth rate assumptions with justification (e.g., based on franchise Item 19 data)
- If a franchise: cite the franchisor's Item 19 financial performance data explicitly

*8.3 — Expense Projections (5 Years)*
- Cost of goods sold (COGS) or cost of service delivery
- Gross profit margin
- Operating expenses by category:
  - Labor / payroll (largest line item for most service businesses)
  - Rent and utilities
  - Royalties and franchise fees
  - Marketing and advertising (franchisor fund + local)
  - Insurance
  - Technology
  - Professional services
  - Miscellaneous / contingency
- EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization)
- Net income after debt service

*8.4 — Non-Marginality Proof Table*
A clear, explicit comparison showing that projected income far exceeds the investor's household living expense need:

| Metric | Year 1 | Year 3 | Year 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Projected gross revenue | $X | $X | $X |
| Net owner income | $X | $X | $X |
| Investor household income need | $X | $X | $X |
| Ratio (net income ÷ household need) | X.Xx | X.Xx | X.Xx |

The ratio should be at least 3x by Year 2–3 to comfortably clear the non-marginality test.

*8.5 — Break-Even Analysis*
- What monthly revenue is required to cover all fixed and variable costs?
- In what month is break-even expected?
- What are the key assumptions driving this timeline?

*8.6 — Cash Flow Projection (Year 1 monthly)*
- Month-by-month cash inflows and outflows for the first 12 months
- Starting cash balance (from working capital investment)
- Ending cash balance each month
- Lowest cash balance point: does the working capital investment cover it?

**SECTION 9: JOB CREATION PLAN (1 page)**
This section stands on its own because it directly addresses the non-marginality test [web:128]:
- List of all planned positions: job title, full-time or part-time, start date, annual wage
- Total projected U.S. jobs created in Year 1
- Total projected U.S. jobs created in Year 3
- Total projected annual payroll in Year 1 and Year 3
- Note: Jobs held by the investor and their family members do NOT count toward job creation

**SECTION 10: INVESTMENT SUMMARY AND E-2 ELIGIBILITY ARGUMENT (1 page)**
- Restate the total investment amount
- Restate that the investment is at risk (funds are committed to the enterprise)
- Confirm proportionality: the investment represents X% of the total cost to establish or acquire this business
- Confirm substantiality: the investment is sufficient to ensure the investor's commitment
- Confirm the investor's Canadian citizenship
- Confirm at least 50% ownership
- Confirm the investor will develop and direct the enterprise
- Confirm the business is not passive investment or a marginal enterprise

## SECTION 4: DEPENDENT APPLICATION DOCUMENTS

### 4.1 Spouse and Children Under 21

Dependents of a valid E-2 holder have a simplified process [page:0]:

**If the principal E-2 visa was issued within the last 6 months:**
1. DS-160 Form (E-1 or E-2 classification)
2. Proof of relationship to the principal: marriage certificate (for spouse) or birth certificate (for children)
3. Copy of the principal E visa holder's visa

**If the principal E-2 visa was issued more than 6 months ago:**
1. DS-160 Form
2. Proof of relationship (marriage certificate / birth certificate)
3. Copy of the principal E visa holder's visa
4. DS-156E of the principal E visa holder
5. Evidence that the business is currently operating — acceptable formats:
   - Monthly bank statements
   - Invoices
   - Profit and loss statements
   - Tax documentation covering the period since the principal's visa was issued

**If the principal converted to E status while in the U.S. (e.g., entered on B-1/B-2 first):**
1. DS-160
2. Proof of relationship
3. Copy of Form I-797 (Notice of Action approving change of status)
4. DS-156E of the principal E visa holder
5. Evidence that the business is currently operating

## SECTION 5: NON-STANDARD CASE DOCUMENTS

### 5.1 Partnership Applications (Two Co-Investors)

When two Canadian investors are co-investing as 50/50 partners, each investor files their own complete E-2 application. However, several documents are shared or require dual treatment:

**Tab C (Ownership) — additional documents needed:**
- Operating agreement clearly showing 50/50 (or stated %) ownership split
- Organizational chart showing both investors and their respective roles
- Passport biodata pages of BOTH investors
- Evidence that each investor's share of the investment comes from their own separate funds (not commingled)

**Tab D (Investment) — additional documents needed:**
- Separate wire transfer / investment documentation for EACH investor's contribution
- The shared business documents (lease, franchise agreement) appear in both binders

**Tab H (Qualifications) — additional documents needed:**
- Separate resumes for each investor
- Separate organizational charts showing each investor's distinct role
- Role differentiation: the two investors must have different, non-overlapping primary responsibilities (e.g., Investor A = Operations Director; Investor B = Business Development Director)
- If the roles appear identical, the officer may question whether both investors genuinely direct the enterprise

**Cover Letter (Tab A) — additional required content:**
- Explicitly states that both investors jointly own at least 50% each in their respective filings
- Describes each investor's specific role and why both roles are necessary
- Confirms that both investors will be physically present and directing the enterprise

---

### 5.2 Adult Child as Co-Investor (Over 21)

When an adult child (21 or older) becomes a co-investor to gain their own E-2 visa, they file as a separate principal investor. The following is required in addition to the standard package:

**Investment Documentation (Tab D):**
- The adult child's investment must be traceable to funds that are genuinely theirs — not a gift from the parent with no real risk or obligation
- If the parent loaned the adult child the funds: a formal promissory note, at market interest rate, secured against the adult child's personal assets — this demonstrates the funds are "at risk" for the adult child
- The adult child's own bank statements showing accumulation of funds

**Ownership Structure (Tab C):**
- Operating agreement showing the specific ownership split (e.g., parent 50%, adult child 50%)
- Organizational chart showing both parties and their distinct directorial roles

**Role Differentiation (Tab H):**
- The adult child's resume and qualifications
- A clear statement of the adult child's specific operational role — it must be distinct from the parent's role
- If the business is too small for two genuine directors, officers may question both applications

**The Separate Application Rule:**
- Each investor (parent AND adult child) files a completely separate E-2 application
- The applications are linked by reference in each cover letter but are evaluated independently
- Each investor's source of funds is traced completely independently

---

### 5.3 Adult Child as Treaty Employee (Not a Co-Investor)

When the adult child applies for an E-2 treaty employee visa under the parent's registered E-2 company:

**Documents required (Tab H equivalent for employee application):**
- Job description clearly showing the executive, supervisory, or essential-skills nature of the role
- Organizational chart showing the adult child's position and their subordinates (executive/supervisory) or their unique specialized function (essential skills)
- Resume demonstrating qualifications for the specific role
- Employment contract or offer letter
- Salary documentation (market-rate compensation)

**For an Executive or Supervisory role:**
- Evidence the role involves high-level decision-making or supervision of multiple employees
- Evidence per 9 FAM 402.9-7(B) criteria

**For an Essential Skills role:**
- Specific documentation of the skill the employee possesses
- Evidence the skill is uniquely essential to the enterprise's operations
- Evidence the enterprise tried and failed to find a qualified U.S. worker
- Projected duration of the essentiality

---

### 5.4 Common-Law Partner as Co-Investor

If a common-law partner is structured as a co-investor (not a dependent spouse), the full co-investor package above applies.

**Important additional document for Tab C:**
- Proof that the common-law partner is a Canadian citizen (passport biodata page)
- The common-law relationship is irrelevant to U.S. immigration — the partner qualifies as an investor in their own right, not as a dependent

**If the couple has since married:**
- Marriage certificate must be included
- The date of marriage matters for dependent eligibility — if married before filing, the spouse qualifies as E-2S

---

### 5.5 Acquisition of an Existing Business (Resale / Non-Franchise)

When the investor is buying an existing operating business rather than a new franchise, additional documentation is required:

**Tab D and Tab E — additional documents:**
- Purchase and sale agreement (signed by both parties)
- Due diligence materials: historical financial statements (3 years), tax returns (2–3 years), business bank statements
- Asset list: what is included in the purchase price
- Proof of transfer of ownership: bill of sale, updated Articles of Organization, new operating agreement

**Tab G (Non-Marginality) — enhanced requirements:**
- Actual historical financial statements (P&L, balance sheet, tax returns) showing the business's real performance
- Explanation of how the investor will maintain or grow that performance
- If the business has been unprofitable: a detailed turnaround plan with credible financial projections and a compelling explanation of why the investor can succeed where the prior owner did not

---

### 5.6 Renewal Application

Renewal applications are submitted to Toronto and require evidence that the business has been operating actively since the last visa issuance [web:121]:

**Required documents for renewal:**
1. MRV fee payment receipt
2. Form DS-160 (current)
3. Form DS-156E
4. Prior visa (expired within the last 12 months, or current visa nearing expiry)
5. First two pages of the last **two years** of federal corporate tax returns (U.S. IRS)
6. Schedule K of the corporate tax return showing ownership percentages, OR an independent auditor's report of financial statements
7. Most recent IRS Form W-2 for all employees AND/OR IRS Form 941 for the past year (proving active payroll)
8. Updated business plan (if the business has changed materially)
9. Evidence of ongoing operation: recent bank statements, invoices, client contracts

**Renewal logic for E2Go:**
- A business that has grown, hired more staff, and increased revenue renews more easily than one that has stagnated
- A business that has consistently paid employees (W-2 / 941 records) demonstrates active operation
- If the business changed category or structure since the last visa, additional explanation is required

## SECTION 6: DOCUMENTS THAT SHOULD NOT BE INCLUDED

### 6.1 What to Leave Out

Including irrelevant, excessive, or harmful documents can weaken an application or trigger unnecessary scrutiny.

**Do NOT include:**
- Unsigned drafts of any document — everything must be fully executed
- Generic business plan templates — must be customized to the investor's specific business and territory
- Social media posts or screenshots as proof of operation (they carry little evidentiary weight)
- Overly optimistic projections without supporting assumptions — officers discount plans that claim Year 1 revenues well above the franchise system's documented Item 19 average
- Personal financial statements from the investor's Canadian life that are not connected to the investment funds
- Letters of intent without corresponding executed agreements — a letter of intent to sign a lease is not a substitute for a signed lease
- Documents that contradict other documents in the binder — internal inconsistency is one of the most common reasons for additional administrative processing (221g)
- Prior denied visa applications or refusal letters — do not include these unless specifically asked
- Excessive padding to fill the binder — the 50-page limit means every page must earn its place

### 6.2 Documents That Are Conditionally Harmful

Some documents help in the right context but hurt if presented incorrectly:

| Document | When it helps | When it hurts |
|---|---|---|
| Signed franchise agreement | Before visa — proves commitment | After visa denial — highlights capital already at risk in a denial |
| Personal bank statements | When clearly showing lawful fund accumulation | When showing insufficient balance AFTER the investment was made |
| Prior Canadian tax returns | Proves lawful source of income | When they show income inconsistent with the claimed investment source |
| Letters from the franchisor | Confirming training, territory, and support | When the letter makes legal claims the investor cannot support (e.g., "guaranteed success") |

# Interview Case Dossier

**E-2 Investor Interview Preparation**

This dossier is designed to help the applicant prepare for an E-2 investor interview by organizing the core facts of the case, likely officer concerns, and the answers the applicant must be able to deliver clearly and consistently. The goal is not to memorize scripts word-for-word, but to understand the business, the investment, and the case theory well enough to answer naturally and accurately.

---

## Candidate Persona

You are a Canadian investor and business operator preparing to actively direct a home care franchise business in Texas. You are not a passive investor; you are the managing member responsible for building the business, overseeing staff, growing referral relationships, supervising operations, and ensuring the company develops into a real and job-creating enterprise.

Your background, management experience, and hands-on role in the business are central to the interview. The officer should come away understanding that you chose this business intentionally, that you understand how it works in practice, and that you are personally essential to its launch and growth.

### Interview Objective

Every answer in the interview should reinforce four core points:

- This is a real business with a real operating plan.
- The investment funds are committed, traceable, and at risk.
- You will actively direct and develop the business.
- The business is positioned to grow, hire workers, and become more than a marginal enterprise.

### Case Theory

The case is strongest when presented as a franchise-backed service business in a growing sector, supported by meaningful capital already spent, a defined operating location, a staffing plan, and an applicant who will personally direct the business rather than merely hold an ownership interest.

That is a stronger framing than simply saying the investment amount is high. The amount matters, but the more persuasive story is that the business is real, the money is committed, and the applicant understands exactly how the business will function and grow.

## Officer Concerns and Response Strategy

This section should prepare the applicant for the most likely pressure points in the interview. For each issue, the applicant should know the concern, the supporting facts, the best short answer, the expanded answer, and what to avoid saying.

### 1. Funds not truly at risk

**Concern:** Some funds may be viewed as not truly spent or not fully committed to the enterprise.

**Facts to know:**

- $132,656 has already been spent or irrevocably committed.
- Funds include franchise fee, office lease costs, furniture, equipment, supplies, and professional fees.
- These expenditures are tied to actual business formation and launch.

**Best short answer:**

> I have already invested $132,656 in business-related expenses including the franchise fee, rent, equipment, furniture, supplies, and professional costs. These funds have been committed to the business and are not easily recoverable.

**Expanded answer:**

> My investment is not just planned; it has already been deployed into the business. I paid the franchise fee, secured office space, purchased equipment and furniture, and incurred professional and setup costs needed to launch operations. These are real business expenses tied directly to the enterprise.

**Avoid saying:**

- Anything vague about money being “clean” or “safe.”
- Anything suggesting funds are still mostly sitting unused.

### 2. Source of funds cannot be traced

**Concern:** The officer may believe the source of funds is not fully documented or that large deposits are unexplained.

**Facts to know:**

- Where the money originated.
- When it was earned or realized.
- When it entered the applicant’s account.
- When it moved into the U.S. business account.
- Which documents prove each step.

**Best short answer:**

> My investment funds came from my personal savings and proceeds from prior asset or business sales, and I can trace the movement of those funds from origin to my business account.

**Expanded answer:**

> I accumulated funds through my work and savings over time, and part of the investment also came from prior asset or business sale proceeds. I can explain the timeline clearly and support it with bank records, transfer records, and the documents relating to the sale that generated the funds.

**Avoid saying:**

- “The funds are clean” without details.
- Anything approximate if exact dates and amounts are available.

### 3. Business appears marginal

**Concern:** The officer may think the business exists only to support the investor and will not create broader economic impact.

**Facts to know:**

- Planned Year 1 hiring.
- Year 5 hiring target.
- Revenue growth path.
- Why the local market supports expansion.

**Best short answer:**

> This business is not intended to support only me. It has a real hiring plan, a service-based growth model, and projected staffing growth from 3-5 caregivers in Year 1 to roughly 12-15 employees by Year 5.

**Expanded answer:**

> The business plan shows a clear path to revenue growth and job creation. We will begin with caregivers and grow the team over time as the client base expands. The goal is to build a sustainable operating company with local employment impact, not a small self-supporting practice.

**Avoid saying:**

- Anything focused only on personal income needs.
- Anything that makes growth sound optional.

### 4. Business plan is generic or inconsistent

**Concern:** The officer may believe the business plan is not specific enough to the market, the business model, or the financial projections.

**Facts to know:**

- Why Dallas and McKinney are suitable markets.
- How the franchise model supports execution.
- What assumptions drive revenue and hiring.

**Best short answer:**

> My business plan is built around a specific franchise model, a defined service area, and a practical operating and hiring strategy for the Dallas and McKinney market.

**Expanded answer:**

> The plan is not a generic concept document. It is based on the Assisting Hands franchise model, the local need for non-medical home care, and the operational steps required to launch and scale in this territory. I understand the assumptions behind the projections and how they connect to staffing, referrals, and service growth.

**Avoid saying:**

- “The lawyers did the plan.”
- Any answer that suggests the applicant has not reviewed the numbers.

### 5. Revenue projections are inflated

**Concern:** The officer may think the projected revenue is too optimistic or unsupported.

**Facts to know:**

- Year 1 and Year 5 projections.
- Why the assumptions are reasonable.
- What drives revenue: clients, service hours, caregiver capacity, and referrals.

**Best short answer:**

> The revenue projections are growth targets based on the service model, staffing plan, and market opportunity, and I understand the assumptions behind them.

**Expanded answer:**

> The projections are tied to business activity rather than guesswork. Revenue depends on building referral relationships, hiring caregivers, and converting client demand into service hours. I can explain how the hiring plan and business development plan support those numbers over time.

**Avoid saying:**

- Anything that implies the numbers are arbitrary.
- Anything inconsistent with the written business plan.

### 6. No credible hiring plan

**Concern:** The officer may question whether the applicant truly knows how staffing will occur.

**Facts to know:**

- Who the first hires will be.
- What roles they will fill.
- How they will be recruited, trained, and managed.

**Best short answer:**

> The business plan begins with 3-5 caregivers in the first year, and I will lead the hiring, training, scheduling, and oversight needed to build the team responsibly.

**Expanded answer:**

> Hiring is a core part of my role. I will recruit local caregivers, ensure they meet the required standards, train and supervise them, and scale staffing in step with client demand. The staffing plan is tied directly to the operating model and expected growth of the business.

**Avoid saying:**

- Vague statements like “we will hire as needed” without specifics.

### 7. Applicant does not understand the business well enough

**Concern:** The officer may think the applicant is repeating prepared lines without genuine operational understanding.

**Facts to know:**

- Services offered.
- Target clients.
- Referral sources.
- Daily operational responsibilities.
- What makes the market attractive.

**Best short answer:**

> I understand the business in detail because I will be the person directing operations, building the team, managing referrals, and overseeing growth.

**Expanded answer:**

> I can explain what services we offer, who our target clients are, how we expect to acquire clients, how caregivers will be hired and managed, and how the business will grow over time. I am not relying on the franchise brand alone; I understand my own responsibilities in operating the business.

**Avoid saying:**

- Generic phrases without examples.
- Anything that suggests someone else will be running the business.

### 8. Business is a shell or has no real operations

**Concern:** The officer may think the business exists only on paper.

**Facts to know:**

- Lease signed.
- Business formed.
- Equipment and furniture purchased.
- Business account opened.
- Any hiring, outreach, or pre-opening steps already completed.

**Best short answer:**

> This is not just a paper entity. We have formed the business, secured office space, invested in setup costs, and taken concrete steps toward operations.

**Expanded answer:**

> The company has a real legal structure, physical office arrangements, capital already deployed, and operational steps underway. I can point to the lease, setup costs, business account activity, and launch preparations as evidence that the business is real.

**Avoid saying:**

- Anything that makes the business sound hypothetical.

### 9. Passive investment concern

**Concern:** The officer may think the applicant is investing money but will not actively direct the enterprise.

**Facts to know:**

- Exact role and authority.
- Day-to-day management duties.
- Why the applicant’s experience matters.

**Best short answer:**

> I will be actively directing the business on a day-to-day basis, including operations, hiring, community outreach, finances, and growth.

**Expanded answer:**

> This is not a passive role. I will supervise caregivers, oversee scheduling and operations, develop referral relationships, manage finances, and guide the overall growth of the company. My management background is part of why I am well-positioned to lead the business.

**Avoid saying:**

- Anything suggesting a partner or staff member will run everything.

### 10. Ownership or control is unclear

**Concern:** The officer may question whether the applicant truly has the authority required to direct the business.

**Facts to know:**

- Ownership percentage.
- Governing documents.
- Management authority.

**Best short answer:**

> I own 50% of the company and serve as the Manager/Managing Member with authority to direct the day-to-day operations of the business.

**Expanded answer:**

> My ownership and control are documented in the company records, including the formation and operating documents. My role is not symbolic; I have an active leadership function and direct authority over how the business is operated.

**Avoid saying:**

- Any percentage other than the actual ownership shown in the file.
- Any answer that conflicts with company documents.

### 11. Interview answers may conflict with the file

**Concern:** Even good facts can be undermined if spoken answers do not match the submitted case materials.

**Facts to know:**

- Ownership percentage.
- Investment amount.
- Service area.
- Dates of major milestones.
- Hiring and revenue projections.

**Best short answer:**

> My answers are based on the same facts reflected in my case file, and I have reviewed the business plan, investment figures, and company documents carefully.

**Expanded answer:**

> I have reviewed the core facts so that my interview answers stay consistent with the application materials. Where there have been updates since filing, I am prepared to explain exactly what changed and when.

**Avoid saying:**

- Guessing.
- Mixing old facts and new facts without clarification.

### 12. Home-country ties are weak

**Concern:** The officer may not be convinced the applicant intends to maintain the required nonimmigrant intent.

**Facts to know:**

- Home in Canada.
- Family ties in Canada.
- Any additional Canadian business, financial, or personal ties.

**Best short answer:**

> I have strong ties to Canada, including my residence and family, and I understand that my E-2 status is temporary.

**Expanded answer:**

> My primary residence and family ties remain in Canada, and I maintain meaningful ongoing connections there. My purpose is to direct the U.S. business under E-2 status, not to abandon my Canadian ties.

**Avoid saying:**

- Anything that sounds like a permanent relocation plan.

**Short answer:**

## Interview Conduct Rules

Strong interview preparation is not only about facts. It is also about how the applicant answers, how consistently the applicant speaks, and whether the applicant sounds credible under pressure.

- Answer the question asked first, then stop.
- Use natural spoken language.
- Keep every number, date, and ownership fact consistent with the file.
- If a fact has changed since filing, explain the change directly.
- Do not guess when exact facts matter.
- Do not exaggerate familiarity if a detail can be reviewed precisely.
- Do not sound defensive or argumentative.
- Do not memorize word-for-word scripts so rigidly that answers sound unnatural.
